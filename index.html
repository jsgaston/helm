Rolling metrics

Monte Carlo

The Monte Carlo view resamples this record's own trade results, with replacement, 2,000 times over the same number of trades. It shows the range of outcomes the same edge could plausibly have produced in a different order. It cannot show what happens if the edge itself changes, which is the larger risk.

Do the bots move together?

Correlation of daily results between magic numbers. Close to +1, two bots win and lose on the same days and add risk together. Close to 0 or negative, they tend to offset each other, which is what keeps the account's drawdown smaller than the sum of its parts.

What you would be buying

Access to the positions of one account, for as long as you subscribe. Not a course, not a bot licence, not advice about what you personally should do with your money.

The entries are automated; the exits are mostly my decisions, made by hand. That makes this record depend on me in a way a fully automated one does not, and it cannot be backtested. The only evidence is the record on this page.

Every figure here comes out of the account automatically. Where a number is missing, it is missing because the account has not produced it yet.

The account is currently a demo account. Demo fills are usually kinder than real ones — no requotes, no partial fills, no slippage on news. Read these figures as a record of how the trades are managed, not as proof of real-money execution. When the account moves to real money this page will say so, and the record will restart.

What can go wrong

When I am away from the screen, open positions stay under the stops and targets the bots set, and my exits can come later than they would otherwise. A manual exit can also be a worse one: judgement has losing streaks too.

Your results will differ from mine. A different broker, spread, execution speed or account size all move the outcome, and a manual close reaches your terminal a moment after it happens on mine.

Ratios such as Sharpe and Calmar are unstable over short periods. Give more weight to drawdown and to the number of months on record.

Leveraged trading can cost you more than you put in. Follow with money you can afford to lose, start at a reduced risk factor, and stop if it stops suiting you.
